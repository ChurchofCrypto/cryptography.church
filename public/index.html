About the Church

The Church of Cryptography is a community dedicated to the sacred art of secure communication and the preservation of privacy in the digital age.

Our history

Founded in the digital realm, we continue the ancient tradition of cryptographers, codebreakers, and privacy advocates who have shaped the course of human history.

Our core beliefs

  • Privacy is a fundamental human right
  • Encryption is the shield of the digital age
  • Knowledge should be free and accessible
  • Security through transparency, not obscurity

Sacred Teachings

  1. IThe first principle: Trust in mathematics, for it is the universal language of truth.
  2. IIThe second principle: In the realm of bits and bytes, privacy is power.
  3. IIIThe third principle: Share knowledge freely, but protect secrets fiercely.
  4. IVThe fourth principle: The strongest walls are built with open source blocks.

Workshops

  • 2 hoursIntro to Zero Knowledge

    A speedrun from introductory zero knowledge to polynomial commitments, PLONK and layered proving systems.

  • 1 hourThe Alberti Cipher: Build It, Use It, Break It

    Build a paper copy of the 40C3 coin's cipher disk, send secret messages, then break them, and learn why “secret” is not “secure”. No laptop needed. Try it now: the Alberti disk.

  • 1 hourHalo2 Tutorial

    Everybody writes a ZK circuit for a simple program. How ZK developed, from theory to smart contracts and beyond, and what its role in our world should be.

  • 1 hourTrustworthy AI

    Federated learning with secure, robust and verifiable aggregation: a neural network whose (limited) trust comes only from cryptography and a majority of N parties, with no central authority.

  • 1 hourGarbled Circuits with Ireul

    Multi-party computation with garbled circuits, using Ireul, an engine that brings together a decade of improvements. Maybe it is time for a comeback.

  • 1.5 hoursFormal Verification Quick-Dive: Proving Alberti's Cipher in Lean

    Learn formal verification by proving the Alberti cipher correct, and see why “proved correct” is not “hard to break”. Preview it now: Proving the Alberti disk.

  • 1 hourDabao Eval Board: Walkthrough + Hello World

    Hands-on with the Baochip Dabao board, compared with other small boards; secure computing at the edge.

  • 1 hourMeshtastic, Reticulum and Friends

    All the cool ways to message your friends off the grid, and the cryptography involved. Devices to try.
